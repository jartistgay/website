1.
Janis lies on their stomach, gazing down the window at the world beyond, seeing only blackness. Occasionally, the rotation of the great machine will show them a single light before it drifts out of view again. Looking up at the belly of the machine, it is much the same. Mostly darkness, a few blinking lights.
Footsteps sound in the corridor. Barely lit, they can see the contour of a face peeking in. “Janis.” The voice they recognise as Inez, their shift leader.
“Yeah?”
“We need you.” People are always asking after Janis. They can never get a moment of rest.
They shift, first getting to their knees, then rising slowly. If they stand too fast, their head will hit the ceiling. They follow Inez through the doorway, careful not to snag their robe on any of the exposed guts of the machine. They stoop slightly for the first few turns, until the corridors start to widen, and they can stand up straight.
“What’s on your mind?” Asks Inez.
They sigh inwardly. “Does it always have to be something?”
Inez leads a snaking path through the corridors. “I’m doing you a favour, Jan.”
What kind of favour, they almost ask, biting their tongue. “I’m just wondering why the Mother decided to give us a view of the world beyond.”
“Sacred sites.” Her voice is clipped, matter-of-fact. “We need places to meditate, to commune with the machine. The Mother is still out there, somewhere, and the windows are the closest we can get to her.”

Oh, that's absolutely something I love in a story, characters who are exactly who they seem. Not that it can't be fun, getting to know someone's backstory or having an unreliable narrator, but there's a very autistic joy in someone being so relentlessly straightforwards, considering that most human being are not.

Also, it's a fun subversion that you just never see. One of my favourite little writing tools is making the reader think that there might be a conflict, and then making them realise, 'actually, that conflict would have been dumb and inconsistent with the characters!'
