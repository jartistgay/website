It's snowing right now.
I've never seen snow before. It's so pretty.
I saw you for the first time today.
You didn't recognise me. Not that I expected it. I've never seen you in person. Only in photographs. And other versions of you.
You've never seen me before. Not in photographs. Not other versions of me.
I felt something in my soul then, of that I'm certain. That my soul called out to yours. Did you feel it?
I miss you terribly.

I lost all my hair. I look like a Dual. It's uncomfortable. I don't know how you stand it all the time. Do you not care at all? Does it not register for you? Or do you endure it, for their sake? For propriety..?

They say that Casters are natural teachers. That breaking down these complex problems in our heads into simple parts makes it easy to analogise, or explain.
I've never felt that I'm a good teacher, personally. But I know that one day I will have to explain everything to you. And I mean everything. That's going to be difficult. So I will practice here.
I'm going to imagine what it will be like, when that day comes. And it will be something like this:

There will come a time several years in the future when you ask me to tell you everything. In reality, I am not now and will never be your sibling; we are perhaps closer to twins, but further than siblings. We don't have the concept of siblings here, but when you leave here and find out what they are, you will take a shining to the concept. You will like to think of me as your sibling. I will wear a blindfold to cover for my lack of sight and my hair will be long and tangled and even though the metal will be gone from my face you will notice all the little pockmarks where it once was. I will sit you down and I will tell you many things. And I will start it like this: “I was not here for these events, nor was the person who told me, nor was the person who told him. This is technically, I guess, now a fourth hand account of these events. How truthful they are, exactly, I'm unsure. I will be imperfect in my remembrance and recounting of what was told to me; the person who told me and the person who told him both are not famous for their veracity…” I will clear my throat. “Where do you want me to start?” And you will say: “Start at the beginning.” And I will say, “I can't exactly start at the very, very beginning. Because the very, very beginning is, well- I mean, I think you know it. It's the birth of the universe; I know a little bit about that, and what brought it about, but not enough to really give you anything more than, ‘the universe started.’ And well, it was a lot of really weird and strange states of matter with lots of subatomic particles floating and colliding around and forming stars in the primordial dust of the universe, et cetera, et cetera, and then all that stuff cooled down, and then it eventually became stars and planets, and it all spread out and- Beyond that, well,” and then I will put a hand to my chin. And you will ask, “Then why not start with the start of humanity? How did humanity get started? And I will say, simply, “Humanity got started. Well, I'm sure you know about evolution. It works a little bit differently now than it did back then. But we were, you know, that our closest genetic relatives are, you know, we are the most closely related to great apes. And that is not to say that we evolved from great apes, but that they are the organisms that we both - we both diverged from the same organism previously. Does that make any sense? No, it doesn't, whatever - you know how evolution works. Humanity came to be born. Africa. It's a very large, very hot continent, or at least, that’s my conception of it. I’ve never been there, obviously. I think it still exists out there, somewhere. A very large continent. It sat on the equator of the planet that humanity was born. The planet humanity was born on was called Earth.” And you will interrupt me, and you will say, “Okay, I was wrong. This is actually kind of boring.” And I will shrug and say, “yes, I assumed so, but I was wondering how long you would let me go on for,” and I will say; “So then, if not the start of humanity, then where?” And you will say “Civilization, perhaps?” And I will say “Not civilization, that is also boring and took thousands of years to develop, besides. We evolved from primitive animals into hunter gatherer societies. Eventually we figured out the division of labor: that different people can do different jobs, and then with that freedom a very small amount of people became craftsworkers and things like that. But still, most of humanity for most of our history were farmers, and they had to farm to create all the food that they needed to survive. And that went on for several thousand years. And then eventually,” and I will put up the hand to my chin again, and I will scratch it. And I will think, and I will say, “Maybe the best place to start is industrialization - when we first discovered, truly discovered, that we can take machines and bend them to our will, that we can work iron or- no, no, no,” I will laugh. I will shake my head and I will smile that big smile that you know so well, that you've seen so many times. But it is not the smile that contains malicious intent. It is the smile of the child. The child that I so often inhabit, and I will say, “No, it started with the book - with the printing press. Well, the printing press was invented multiple times but it didn't really take off until what they would have called around the thirteen hundreds.” And you will say, “It's the thirteen hundreds right now.” And I will say, “Indeed, it is. What a strange coincidence, especially given that where I plan to actually start the story also begins with a similar coincidence of two similar dates. Sort of coinciding.” And you will say, “You had planned a starting point. Why did you not start there?” And I will say, “Well, you had other questions. And you wanted me to start at the start of well, everything,” and I will wave a hand and you will wave a hand and we will both laugh. And I will say, “It’s never bad to want more information. You will just have to forgive me, for I am horrid at explaining. All right, I guess, now, I'm going to skip the very, very beginning, but I will start where I planned.” “And that place is where?” I will shrug and I will say, “you know, of the Gods right?” And you will say, “of course, I have met a few,” and I will nod and say, “yes, yes, well, we're going to start a little bit before they were born. Fifty years before they were born, and then we'll skip ahead a little bit. And then, and then we'll talk about the Gods, because, well, we don't call them - we call them the Gods for a good reason. You know, everything really started with them. So:” and I will clear my throat, and I will limber up and stretch, and I will fix my eyes on yours, and I will say, I will say: “It all began with the gods.”
