Saint Bernard
I said, "Make me love myself, so that I might love you".

Laura isn’t happy.
Why should she be? The world is fake, after all, fake! And she should be shouting it from the rooftops. Wasn’t there a movie about this? Doesn’t matter now; whatever dusty VHS tape she scrounges up will be just as fake as her. Simulacra.
She has a poor habit of picking at her lips, always has, ever since she was a kid, and she applies lip balm assiduously, but when she runs out or loses her tube the habit continues without her notice, even if it’s been years, and she will pick her lips bloody and raw with her fingers and her tongue and her teeth.
She’s out of lip balm, now.
And she’s drunk.
“You’re practically eating your own face,” Natalie says, pushing her sister’s hand down somewhat gently.
“Mnmn?” She looks up and her eyes are hazy and unfocused. “Nat?”
“No, it’s not me,” she says, “I’m not in Iceland, and I haven’t tracked you down. Because Iceland was turned into plasma. And so were you and I. That’s what you'd say, right?”
Laura wants to get out of this situation. Have her delicious whiskey in peace. Drink through it. Drink through it. “Drink through it.”
“Drink through it,” Natalie nods, and she sits and taps the table, shave-and-a-hair-cut.
Two-, Laura raps out, necessarily removing her hand from her whiskey in the process, and before she reaches -knocks, Nat has already stolen her glass and downed it, ice and all. “Ahnn da hrocgks?” She asks through the comically oversized cubes that remain in her mouth. She spits them back into the glass.
“I am a way about temperatures,” Laura says, and Nat recognises the phrase as one of her own construction.
“You are a way,” she agrees.
“Maybe Iceland still exists,” she suggests, feeling the tears and acid rise in her throat, “And you and I are there right now, by some,” she gropes the air with an arm - no bangles. “Some, some miracle sort.”
“How many atoms in the human body? Within an order of magnitude.”
“Ten to the twenty-seven. Seven times ten to the twenty-seven. Less, we’re both short. Six, six-and-a-half.”
Nat tilts her head. “You’re clearly not drunk enough.”
“Not drunk enough for what?” Laura is plucked tight as a bowstring, because if she needs to be drunk enough for Nat, for her sister Natalie to show her something, then it’s gotta be something big, like ‘get everyone out of this fucking place’, big.
“I’ll show you when you’re drunk enough.” She waves the bartender over and orders Laura another glass of her whiskey, for herself a glass of Engine (neat), and two shots of something nasty, a bottom-shelf vodka they used to drink back in college, one for each of them. When he returns with four separate articles of glass, he sets them down gently and the moment his back is turned, Natalie downs both shots and half her Engine.
“Christ, Nat.” Laura is chewing at her face again absently.
Nat, for her part, takes it like a champ. “There are many things in this world you don’t understand, Laura. For one, how to live your life. You never knew, from the moment we lost mum, you had no idea. That day when you saw her leave you attached a fishing hook to her and you followed that line, hand over hand, wherever you thought it was leading you. You weren’t living.” She swallows the bad taste in her mouth with another sip of gin. “Do you catch my cold, Laura?” Laura kinda sorta opens her mouth to maybe say something, but Natalie’s built up so much steam, there’s no stopping her. “God, it’s insane! I mean I know I’m no fucking paragon of mental health, but you were a goddamned magnet drawn inexorably towards the north fucking pole and the second Earth’s magnetic field disappeared you acted like there weren’t any other magnetic fields anywhere in the universe! I mean, have you even read Nietzsche?” She hasn’t, but- “GOD IS DEAD, AND WE HAVE KILLED HIM!” She bellows, and even before that she was loud enough that the bartender was getting a little worried about his only two patrons. “Just because there’s no finding mum again, just because the world,” she gestures, “the world is the way it is, doesn’t mean-”
And finally Laura cuts her off.
“The world is abso-fucking-lutely not the way it is, Nat, it’s the way you made it! God is not dead, you’re him, and you made the world in your image, and now you’re chastising me for not liking your image!”
“You would’ve done things differently,” Natalie says, leaning back.
It’s a trap, and she knows it. “Big surprise. Of course I would’ve.”
Nat baits her even further, “How? What? What would you have done?”
“I don’t know, Nat…”
“You know. I’d bet everything that you haven’t been able to stop thinking about what you would’ve done.”
Laura denies it, because it’s true. “Of course not. What’s done is done. I’ve tried to crack the system we’re in, but…”
Natalie screws up her mouth and then loosens it with the last of her glass. “Yeah. Done.”
“That’s also bait,” Laura says aloud, “You’re leading me on, trying to make me believe that there’s some loophole. That you made the same mistake as the Wheel, even though I know you’re not stupid, Nat, you’re smarter than every one of those feckless cowards put together. There’s no loophole. There’s no backdoor.”
“Can’t fault a girl for trying.” Natalie shrugs and stands, and all the alcohol in her blood rushes to her brain; she manages not to sway on her feet. “Good luck, Laura. Take what I said to heart.”
Something unnameable builds inside Laura, whatever it was that propelled her all those years, some combustible fuel, and she tugs on her sister’s coat as she’s leaving. “Nat.”
She’s looking down because she can’t meet Natalie in the eye, and Natalie can’t help but wrap an arm around her. “Laura.”


“I’ve always thought you should live in an alley,”
Natalie begins conversationally.
Laura laughs, cheeks pink, condensation pluming from her mouth as she crouches slightly to match the key to the lock. “You’ll love it even more inside. Come, look.” She turns the handle and they filter in. The place is tiny, square, maybe twenty feet by twenty, or twenty-five, with a single curtained window and a vestibule-sized bathroom. Laura shucks off both boots with the sole of the other and leaves them on the muddy towel, hangs her peacoat on the rack which already overflows. Natalie matches, except she unlaces her own boots manually, and the sweater doesn’t come off, not until Laura’s cranked the baseboard heater, and she’s had enough time to warm up and examine her sister’s… home?
“Home?” She asks casually.
“Home.” Laura gestures to the myriad posters, the shelf set into the wall which sags with the weight of onionskin magic textbooks. Her old ones, from college. She couldn’t bear to throw them away. “Here.” She pulls a flask from the fridge, a little thing maybe a metre in height at best, faux-wood grain design something out of the fifties. “The thing is so old,” she prefaces with a wide sweep of both arms, outwards, “well,” she looks at the flask, encrusted with frost. “I have to scrape ice off the coolant lines about once a month. Twice, in winter. Anything I leave in there grows a little overcoat.”
She passes it to Nat, who has a bit of trouble opening it, until the frost breaks off, and she takes a generous swig. “Eugh! What is this stuff.”
Laura rousts a plastic baggy - like it has weed in it, or something - from somewhere, with little prickly green insides. “Juniper.”
“Jeez, Laura.” Natalie drinks again.
“It’s cold,” laughs Laura.
“I’ll give you that,” she says, still reeling from her second sip. “It’s cold.”
Laura sits at the crappy wooden chair at the desk, slapping her keyboard. The computer wakes, its three monitors (one horizontal, one vertical, one an ancient five-by-four she must’ve pulled from an e-waste pile) waking up, each on significant delay from the last. Natalie still holds the flask, watching her sister. She types in her password. New, just one letter. “Here,” she mumbles, booting up a music player. “Get some background.”
Her face has darkened in only the minute it took to put something on. “This good?”
It’s something slower, jazzy. Maybe blues? Nat’s never been one for music. “It’s good,” she permits. Laura rotates the chair and sits hunched over, knees on elbows. Natalie takes a drink out of compulsion, and nervousness. She holds it out to Laura, who removes it from her possession.
“Good heater,” she comments, removing her sweater.
“Jeez, Nat.”
“What? I’m wearing a bra.” A little sports bra - never like she needed (or preferred) much more than that. Her chest is small enough that she often goes without, and nobody is any the wiser.
Laura holds the flask like it’s her only tether to sanity. “I have t-shirts.”
“So?” Natalie shrugs. “There’s a certain romanticism to a room like this. The winter is so cold, yet we’re all here, warm and toasty. Usually works better with a fireplace, though,” she laughs, “and not a baseboard heater. But it works well enough.”
Laura crumples more. “Please put a shirt on, Nat.”
“We’re twins!” She protests, laughing. “It’s nothing you haven’t seen before!”
Laura glares at her through her fingers.
“Whaat?” She asks. Natalie tilts her head. “Oh my God, are you into… yourself?!?” She laughs, and Laura doesn’t move. “Ok, not funny?” She stands and crosses the two foot gap between them, crouching down to her sister’s level. “Laura. C’mon.”
“I’m druuuuuunk,” moans Laura. “Nat?” She pleads.
“Not happening,” she proclaims proudly, shucking her skirt and fur-lined leggings. “Gotta keep the socks, though,” she says. “Drink through it.” She taps the flask still in Laura’s hands and checks the fridge for anything else - three old beers in a one of those cardboard carrying things, each a different label. She selects one at random and eyes anything she can use as a bottle opener - ah, a plastic lighter, on Laura’s desk. She jams it between the knuckle of her thumb and the cap, venting some of the pressure. “Ugh, it’s been a minute,” she complains.
“Here.” Laura takes the objects from her hands, applying them properly.
The beer foams up enough that Natalie has to put her mouth on the end. She coughs, nasally, “yuck, it went up my nose.” She chugs about a third and stands with her hands on her hips, staring at nothing in particular.
Laura takes the moment to examine her sister. Yes, they’re twins. No, she cannot hide the thrill that runs through her when she sees her sister, barely dressed. Just panties and a sports bra. The panties have been sewn up and patched multiple times, their orange stripes pulled unevenly here and there. Nat turns to look out the window at the snow falling, and Laura gets a good view of her butt. It’s cute. Of course it is. She sighs.
"Hey,” Natalie says, flicking the switch. “You have a light for out there.”
“Yeah,” Laura sighs, trying to finish off the homemade gin, and failing. “Door’s behind the desk, though.”
“But you move it,” observes Natalie, pointing at little lines scarred into the floor (also faux-wood) by the legs of her desk.
Laura grips the lighter, holding it up for inspection.
“Hmm? Oh,” she examines Laura’s desk further. A pack of reds. “You’re smoking again.”
“Yeah.”
“Shoulda connected the dots.” She shrugs. “Let’s have a smoke, then.”
Laura frowns. “Since when did you smoke?”
“Since Uni,” she admits, a final weakness. “I just never told you because I didn’t want to encourage it. I knew if we both smoked, we’d always be out there, puffing away. Instead of working, or being at the party, you know?”
“I know.”
“Well, there’s no party here now.” Natalie pulls one, tucking the butt between her lips. “What’s the harm? None of this is real, right?”
“Iceland is plasma now,” Laura agrees.
“But you have to match me,” she laughs, speaking around the cigarette. “I can’t be out in the snow naked.”
“So you want me to be, too?” She raises an eyebrow.
“It’ll be fun,” coaxes Natalie.
She doesn’t need to ask a second time. Laura would strip for her sister in any weather. In Antarctica. During Abstract War. She’s only wearing a tight undershirt, that stupid waffle fabric that she hates but can’t deny the effectiveness of. And the shirt comes off, revealing nothing underneath. (Natalie whistles in response.) And off come the pants, too, just tight boxers underneath.
“You never struck me as a boxers gal.” Nat elbows her.
“They’re Nick’s,” she admits reluctantly. “Were Nick’s. Sometimes I wouldn’t have time for laundry, and so I’d… yeah. They’re comfortable. And a lot warmer than panties, in this weather,” she says, elbowing her sister back.
“Yeah, yeah, help me move your stupid desk.”
They haul the desk out of the way and step into the snow, lit dimly by an incandescent orange bulb, so incandescent that Natalie assumes it to be sodium vapour, before she realises that it can’t be. Laura lights up, and takes a deep drag, then another; passes it. They’re already both shivering.
“God, your nipples,” laughs Nat.
“You cannot talk about my nipples,” says Laura stiffly, voice deeper from the exhale. “I’m serious, Nat.”
“Oh, c’mon.”
“I’m literally freezing my tits off here, Nat. This is not a joking matter.” Her teeth chatter on the last bit as she passes.
“Literally,” she says, grinning. She sticks it in between her teeth and sucks in frigid air so she can maintain the grin.
“Like you're any better,” Laura snaps. “I can see them through your stupid bra.”
“Oh?” Nat laughs, covering her nipples with the tips of her fingers. “I didn’t know you were such a prude, Laura Ferno-”
Laura presses her torso to her sister’s, leeching out blissful warmth. “Oh, Jesus,” she says.
“Mmm,” Nat agrees, turning her face to blow away smoke from the corner of her mouth. “Warm indeed.” She passes the cigarette back and Laura melts into her, head on her shoulder, arm looped under hers to hold the cig to her lips. Natalie rubs the skin of her sister’s shoulder. “You’re so pale,” she says. “Iceland will not make a wight out of my sister yet.”
Laura laughs, and Natalie can feel it reverberating through her own chest cavity. “I try to get sun. But it’s so hard this far north.”
Nat forces their bodies to separate, holding out her arm for inspection. “Look how much darker I am than you.”
“Nat, you live in England.”
“Laura, it’s not, ‘I live in England and I have no right to say what I said’, it’s ‘I live in England and I'm getting more sun than you’!”
Laura groans. “Sue me.” She bites into the filter. It’s burned down enough that Nat’s fine with her killing it, but this is something else. “Ra is here.”
“Ra is not here.”
“Ra built this,” She gestures widely at the little stone courtyard filled with snow. “Ra built everything here. But most of all?” She points towards Krallafjöll. “Ra is there.”
“We’re a six hour drive from Krallafjöll, Laura.”
Laura’s eyes are haunted. “I can’t get closer.”
“What?”
“I can’t get closer without seeing Ra’s mana, without seeing Benj and the glass man, his molybdenum ring, without seeing you, and me, in that volcano, and-”
“And you killing him.”
“Yeah,” she breathes, and now they’re only out, freezing their tits off, because drunk and on the verge of hypothermia, frostbite, it’s the only way they can- “Stop killing people. That’s what you told me that day.” She wipes away tears. “I still don’t remember waking up. We were in Tanako’s world, and at some point we weren’t, at least I think so? But what if we’re still there.” She holds onto her sister’s shoulders, pushing and pulling her. “What if we’re still in Tanako’s world?”
Natalie swallows. “We’re not in Tanako’s world, because Tanako’s world doesn’t exist anymore. We were there when Ra destroyed it. You don’t have life insurance anymore, Laura. You die, you die.”
“That’s not what I mean.” Her teeth are chattering so bad now. “There were so many layers, after a point. T-world, Māyā, the Listening Post, all of it, so many mes, so many Ras… what if I just… got lost?” Her voice is so light, airy, the consistency of tiramisu. “And one day,” she walks her fingers along Nat’s arm, up across her clavicle, over her heart. “I’ll wake up.”
“Sometimes,” Natalie confesses, “I still think I’m in Abstract War. I’ll wake up from a dream, and, and-” she’s crying, and can’t bear to bring herself to finish. “You can’t imagine what Abstract War was like.”
“I can.” She shrugs, “I’m sorry Nat, but I’ve fought Ra.”
“You didn’t survive Ra. There was no fight. Not until Ashburne - mum.” She shakes her head. “Mum was a badass. I wish you could’ve seen her.”
“I did, enough.” She presses her face into the crook of Natalie’s neck. “I wish you could’ve met her. I wish she could’ve seen what you were capable of. Are capable of.”
