jun ?, 2026 - tubular logic postmortem
Whoo! Finally finished. This is a story I've had cooking for quite a long time now... I want to say 2021, but probably even before that. My earliest notes date it April 2023, but that was the last iteration it went through before it became what you know now. Before anything else, though, it's time to lay my inspirations on the table.
Fun fact, but the station has a name, even though it never really comes up. It's called Galfridian, as an homage to High Noon Over Camelot, which also features a tube shaped space station. (Often cited as an O'Neill cylinder, but unlikely considering no official art I was able to find shows the concentric rings that would actually classify it as such. (Also contradicted by the narrative, lore, and OOC statements from the cast, but if I litigated those we'd be here all day.))
I'm actually unsure if Fort Galfridian was the main inspiration behind this story. It certainly shares a lot of the same ideas, featuring two civilisations, one on the inside of the tube and one in the walls, with the technology that built the station having passed from memory. But I could've sworn the original idea came from a video by John Michael Godier or Isaac Arthur. Nevertheless, the mix of 'high' and 'low' technologies[1] is one of my lifelong obsessions, which was only truly bolstered by HNOC.
Some of the main implementations of Galfridian, especially near the end, were heavily inspired by Adam Oyebaji's Braking Day, and the spaceships featured in that novel. (And some other stuff I can't mention without spoiling it.) I highly recommend it to anyone who enjoys hard SF, fun (and grim) explorations of AI, or wanted something like The Martian, but further in the future.
Also, of course, props go to my favourite novel of all time, and the single biggest cornerstone for me as an artist: The Worthing Saga. (Obligitory disclaimer that Orson Scott Card is a bigot, and you shouldn't give him money, but this book is out of print, so I don't feel too bad praising it as much as I do.) This was truly the birth of my passion for mixing SF with Fantasy, and the fun ways in which that story tackles the long course of human history is genuinely unparalleled. I still remember the first time I read that book, I was very young (~10), and I was blown away by the fact that the main character was a medieval farm boy, who, nonetheless, knew what spaceships were. (Also worth reading are Hot Sleep and Capitol, both earlier takes on the same narrative by the same author, containing some content cut for The Worthing Saga.)
Next up is another OSC book, and one more in line with the themes of Tubular Logic: the Pathfinder trilogy. If you liked the idea of a bunch of peasants discovering that the people who came before them were far more advanced, and working to understand their technology, then you should probably read/watch Dr. Stone. But if you, like I, have a bone to pick, or think it's just a little too anime-y, then pick up this trilogy, and maybe drop it when you feel it's getting too much for you. I'd recommend stopping after the first book, but it does end on something of a cliffhanger, and the latter entries are far more conceptually interesting than they are good novels, per se. Trust me, if you drop them anywhere after the first 50 pages of Ruins, then you're not missing out on a lot.
Scattered mentions also go to: TaZ: Balance, Girls' Last Tour, 100 Years of Solitude, The Stormlight Archive, Piranesi, Ars Paradoxica, and, of course, my beloved Hunter x Hunter.
Hunter x Hunter is one of the stories I've spent the most time thinking about over the course of my life. Give the manga a read, or the 2011 version a watch. When I first saw it, I was fascinated by the breadth and depth of the world. A story that started with a typical psuedo-magical anime setup blends into a mafia war in New York City, blends into a video game isekai, blends into so many other things. My watching it was the catalyst for developing the world of 3 Dimensional Swans, where Tubular Logic takes place.
I'm not sure how much I can really say about 3DS and how it connects to Tubular Logic without spoiling some of my ideas for the main storyline. None of the characters here will probably connect to that story, I don't think, but Galfridian is something of an important place, historically. It was very fun to loosely tie everything together in the way that I did; I refrained from touching any of the big ideas or setpieces in that world. You may have noticed I never really described their towns or what the inside of the station looks like at all. This is why. Tubular Logic takes place on the periphery of all the stuff I had previously planned. Anyway, that's enough rambling about inspirations. We're moving on to what I actually learned writing this thing.

So, first and foremost, this was a story I didn't really have to edit, which was a new experience for me. I'd say it's because I had so much of it in mind before I started, but I really didn't. Almost everything was by the seat of my pants; it wasn't until I started to introduce Regina that I knew I wanted it to be a generational story.
