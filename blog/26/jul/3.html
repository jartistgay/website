jul 3, 2026 - 25 and Tubular Logic
I'm so glad to finally see this one on the website! This was the first thing I wrote after my novel that I was genuinely proud of, probably because it took an obscene amount of work to get everything right. Special shoutout to my friend Nate who provided a lot of copy editing and suggestions for phrases that sounded a little more natural. Also thanks to everyone who read it and provided feedback (which I used for my advertisement on bluesky lol).
I'm thinking of doing a weird sort of comments section for future stuff. I tend to discuss my art a lot with friends during all stages of the process (planning, writing, editing). And they add on a lot, ask questions that clarify my ideas, etc, and I thought that would be interesting to show to the audience. I really don't want to tell people how to interpret my art, but providing a place for people to bounce ideas back and forth is really what I want to encourage here. I think a lot of artistic discussion happens behind closed doors (discord etc) and that's often for good reason, but as someone who can't stand discord servers, this is a fun alternative.
The only problem? Well, comments sections are hard to implement. Even free options are really annoying for one reason or another, and on top of that, the design of whatever service I use would clash heavily with the style of my site. So, given that currently almost nobody looks at this website, it would probably be manageable for me to hand-code the comments. Is it stupid? Absolutely. But for all the reasons listed above, this is what I am going to do, going forwards. So please, if you have something interesting to say, shoot me an email: j@jartist.gay. I'm already preparing a comments section for Tubular Logic, which I still hope to have up by next week. Please check back next friday.
Speaking of, Tubular Logic! This is another story I'm very excited for. (I mean, sharing art is scary, so I wouldn't be sharing it if I didn't like it.) The biggest reason, besides that it's quite good, is that it takes place in the 3DS universe (shared with my novel), and getting to show people the many different sides to that world is a great joy. The story itself is basically finished, I just need to hear back from one of my beta readers, to fill out the aforementioned comments section. And also, having a few weeks in between completing something and publishing it means that I have enough distance from it to be able to catch most major errors.
Sorry if this post is a bit scatterbrained. I'm not rushing it, per se, but I have something coming up later today, so I'm not putting as much care into organising my thoughts. Also another thing about 3DS: I'm currently writing another short story in that universe, one that acts as a sort of intro/explainer as to its history and a lot of the big ideas underpinning it. I'm hoping it doesn't end up being too long, but there's a solid chance that it's just going to turn into its own novel. The good thing is that I'm not setting a minimum or maximum word count; whatever comes out of my pen is what you'll get.
(Oh, and, I'm writing another short story in this universe, set in the same place that Paracosm is set. The tentative title is Everything Must Go!. After Tubular Logic, this is most likely what you're going to be seeing next.)
Also weird final note: I'm thinking of hosting my novel on this website. It'll take an obscene amount of work just in the formatting alone, but that story is kind of my baby, and I really don't want to compromise my vision in any way. Unfortunately for you, you're not going to see it for a very long while. Paracosm is only really the intro for an enormous story that I've planned for a long time, and I don't want to release anything publicly until I have a good portion of it done. I just don't want to have any enormous hiatuses, that's all. And I think that more people are going to read something if it's got a regular (and frequent) update schedule, instead of having enormous gaps in between chapters. Just know that when it comes, it's going to be amazing.
And finally, you may be asking, what about Six Corners? I still really want to do it, but I hit a brick wall, one I'm not very interested in scaling at the moment. I just have other projects that I'm way more excited for, and that are far more likely to be committed to long term (or short term, for the shorter projects).

Thanks for reading my ramble! Peace.
